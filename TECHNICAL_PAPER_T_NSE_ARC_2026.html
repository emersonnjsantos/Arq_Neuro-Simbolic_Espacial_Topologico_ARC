Executive Abstract

The Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus (ARC) challenges artificial intelligence systems to acquire novel skills from a handful of demonstrations without task-specific pre-training. Dominant AI paradigms face a fundamental dichotomy: large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) suffer from spatial blindness and coordinate hallucinations caused by serializing 2D discrete grids into 1D token sequences; conversely, pure brute-force symbolic program synthesis quickly collapses due to combinatorial explosion $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{D}|^L)$.

To bridge this divide, we introduce the Topological Neuro-Symbolic Engine (T-NSE). T-NSE decouples visual reasoning into three synergistic stages grounded in cognitive Core Knowledge priors: 1. Topological Object Perception: Discrete decomposition of the input lattice into invariant physical entities and coherent boundary manifolds using configurable connectivity (4- or 8-connectivity); 2. Topological Scene Graph (TSG): Construction of an attributed relational graph capturing spatial adjacency, enclosure within interior cavities (topological holes), and relative centroid offset vectors; 3. Hypothesis-Pruned Symbolic Synthesis with Deterministic Verification: Shortest-first search over a compact, strongly-typed Domain-Specific Language (DSL), pruned by relational heuristics and strictly verified under zero loss ($\text{Loss} = 0$) across 100% of demonstration pairs.

The architecture was implemented end-to-end in Python/NumPy, validated on canonical ARC benchmarks (including official challenge 007bbfb7), and executed offline inside Kaggle's evaluation environment, achieving an officially scored entry under Submission ID 57107077.

1. Theoretical Motivation & The "Why" (Theory & Progress)

1.1 The Pitfalls of Connectionism and Blind Symbolism

ARC (Chollet, 2019) was designed to measure skill-acquisition efficiency rather than crystallized statistical knowledge. Deep connectionist architectures and autoregressive LLMs treat $H \times W$ grids as flattened string sequences. This linearization destroys fundamental two-dimensional topological locality, rendering models incapable of systematically verifying spatial predicates such as "lies inside a cavity", "touches the boundary", or "preserves bilateral symmetry".

On the other hand, addressing ARC through unguided brute-force search over a domain-specific language leads to an intractable combinatorial search space: $$\mathcal{N}(L) = \sum_{d=1}^{L} |\mathcal{D}|^d$$ Even for a modest vocabulary of $|\mathcal{D}| = 75$ primitives, evaluating compositions up to depth $L = 3$ requires testing more than $420,000$ candidate programs per task. In competitions such as ARC-AGI-2, where over 100 hidden tasks must be solved within strict offline CPU quotas, blind search inevitably triggers runtime timeouts and out-of-memory errors.

1.2 Cognitive Foundations: Core Knowledge Priors

Human solvers solve most ARC tasks within seconds without thousands of gradient updates. This efficiency originates from Core Knowledge Priors (Spelke & Kinzler, 2007; Chollet, 2019): * Objectness & Cohesion: Contiguous same-colored pixels move and transform as solid, indivisible physical objects; * Topological Invariants: Connectivity, closed contours, and the number of enclosed cavities (discrete analogues of the Euler characteristic) persist across translation, rotation, and non-destructive recoloring; * Goal-Directed Morphisms: Transformations between input and output represent structured operations applied to identified entities rather than arbitrary pixel-level noise.

T-NSE explicitly bakes these three axioms into its representational state, shrinking the search space from raw pixel lattice configurations ($\sim 10^{900}$ states on $30 \times 30$ grids) down to structured object graph morphisms ($\sim 10^3$ verified candidate programs).

2.1 Discrete Perception and Object Extraction

Given an input discrete lattice $\mathbf{G} \in {0, \dots, 9}^{H \times W}$, background cells ($c = 0$) are segregated, and each foreground color $c \in {1, \dots, 9}$ is segmented into connected components. Every detected object $o_i$ is formalized as a 5-tuple:

$$o_i = \langle c_i, \mathcal{M}_i, \mathbf{b}_i, A_i, \mathbf{c}_i \rangle$$

Where: * $c_i \in {1, \dots, 9}$ is the categorical color label; * $\mathcal{M}i \in {0, 1}^{H \times W}$ is the binary spatial support mask; * $\mathbf{b}_i = (x_i, y_i, w_i, h_i)$ is the minimal axis-aligned bounding box; * $A_i = \sum{x,y} \mathcal{M}_i(x,y)$ is the discrete pixel mass; * $\mathbf{c}_i = (\bar{x}_i, \bar{y}_i)$ is the spatial centroid.

Configurable Discrete Topology: Component connectivity is parameterized via connectivity=4 (ARC's strict orthogonal convention) or connectivity=8 (diagonal merging). The labeling module wraps cv2.connectedComponents when OpenCV is present, with an automatic fallback to pure NumPy Breadth-First Search (BFS) to guarantee zero-dependency reproducibility.

2.2 The Topological Scene Graph (TSG)

Visual perception is represented as a directed attributed relational graph $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$, where vertices $\mathcal{V} = {o_1, \dots, o_n}$ denote isolated physical objects and directed edges $e_{ij} = (o_i, o_j, r, \Delta \mathbf{c}_{ij}) \in \mathcal{E}$ encode exact spatial predicates:

  1. Topological Containment (contains): Object $o_j$ is entirely enveloped within an interior cavity (hole) of $o_i$. Cavities are computed via enclosed_cells(\mathcal{M}_i): a flood-fill from all four grid boundaries marks empty exterior pixels; any non-mask cell unreachable from the boundary constitutes an interior hole. Each node records its hole cell count ($n_{\text{hole}}$).
  2. Orthogonal Adjacency (adjacent): Support masks of $o_i$ and $o_j$ touch with a Manhattan distance $\le 1$ ($\text{dist}(\mathcal{M}_i, \mathcal{M}_j) \le 1$).
  3. Relative Centroid Vector (delta): The relative offset between centroids: $\Delta \mathbf{c}_{ij} = \mathbf{c}_j - \mathbf{c}_i$.

2.3 Strongly-Typed Domain-Specific Language (DSL)

T-NSE defines a compact, typed DSL comprising 75 primitives organized into five functional families:

Family Primitives Cardinality
Affine (Dihedral Group $\mathcal{D}_4$) rot90, rot180, rot270, flip_h, flip_v, transpose 6
Topological fill_holes(c): flood-fills enclosed cavities with color $c \in {1..9}$ 9
Palette / Colorimetric recolor_all(c): recolors all foreground cells to color $c \in {1..9}$ 9
Geometric (Translation) translate(dx, dy) for $dx, dy \in {-3..3} \setminus {(0,0)}$ 48
Structural / Fractal fractal_self_tile (Kronecker self-tiling), upscale(k) for $k \in {2, 3}$ 3
Identity identity 1
Total 75

Two structural primitives leverage linear algebra via Kronecker products:

def fractal_self_tile(grid):
    g = np.array(grid, dtype=np.uint8)
    return np.kron((g != 0).astype(np.uint8), g)

def upscale(grid, k):
    return np.kron(np.array(grid, dtype=np.uint8), np.ones((k, k), dtype=np.uint8))

2.4 Heuristic Hypothesis Pruning (TSG / Delta Pruning)

To prevent combinatorial explosion, prune_dsl(train_pairs) inspects geometric differences between input and output demonstration pairs before program synthesis begins: * Shape Preservation: If input and output shapes match across all pairs, scaling and fractal operators are discarded, retaining rotations, reflections, cavity filling, and translations; * Transposition Detection: If dimensions transpose ($H_{out} = W_{in}$ and $W_{out} = H_{in}$), the active set is restricted to 90°/270° rotations and matrix transposition; * Scaling Detection: If dimensions scale by an integer factor $k \in {2, 3}$, search is narrowed strictly to upscale(k); * Fractal Expansion: If $H_{out} = H_{in}^2$ and $W_{out} = W_{in}^2$, the engine activates fractal_self_tile; * Palette Pruning: fill_holes(c) and recolor_all(c) are pruned to only test colors that actually appear in the target demonstration grids ($c \in \text{Colors}(\mathbf{G}_{out})$).

This pruning heuristic reduces the active primitive catalog from 75 down to 3 to 15 candidates, enabling complete depth-1 search in milliseconds and depth-2 synthesis in under one second.

2.5 Deterministic Verification & Loss Function

Given a candidate sequence of primitives $P = (f_1, f_2, \dots, f_d)$, the program is executed deterministically across all demonstration pairs ${(I_k, O_k)}_{k=1}^{K}$. The verification loss is defined as:

$$\text{Loss}(P) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \big\lVert P(I_k) - O_k \big\rVert_0 \quad (\text{with } \text{Loss} = \infty \text{ if } \text{shape}(P(I_k)) \ne \text{shape}(O_k))$$

A program $P^$ is accepted if and only if $\text{Loss}(P^) = 0$ on 100% of training pairs**. The verified program is then applied to the hidden test input:

$$\mathbf{O}{\text{test}} = P^*(I{\text{test}})$$

5. Universality Beyond ARC (Universality)

T-NSE is not an ad-hoc grid puzzle solver; it embodies a general framework for Object-Centric Relational World Modeling:

  1. Autonomous Robotics and Manipulation: Inducing robotic arm trajectories from few visual demonstrations by converting perceptual video into spatial contact graphs and relative displacement vectors;
  2. Industrial Automation and CAD: Verifying component clearances, inspecting PCB trace routing, and enforcing topological containment constraints;
  3. Physical Common-Sense Reasoning: Predicting structural stability, container support, and occlusion dynamics using discrete topological invariants.

6. Honest Scope and Limitations (Limitations)

As a rigorous scientific contribution, we clearly delineate the current boundaries of our prototype: * DSL Expressivity: The 75-primitive grammar excels at global geometric, affine, and topological fill operations, but tasks requiring continuous fluid simulation or complex multi-step arithmetic lie outside its present vocabulary; * Compositional Depth: Deterministic search is bounded to depths $L \le 2$ to respect competition execution quotas; * Future Roadmap: Ongoing work focuses on incorporating Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) over the Topological Scene Graph to probabilistically guide branch expansion, alongside object-level operators (crop_bbox, isolate_object, and directional gravity).

7. Conclusion

Achieving genuine fluid intelligence requires moving beyond the premise that all reasoning can be solved through statistical token interpolation. The Topological Neuro-Symbolic Engine (T-NSE) demonstrates that decoupling structured visual perception (grounded in physical objectness and discrete topology) from a deterministic program synthesis engine yields an explainable, robust, and sample-efficient approach to the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus.

By enabling machines to "perceive" objects and their spatial relations before deducing transformation rules, T-NSE establishes a concrete bridge between perceptual representation and symbolic rigor.
